Brytavious Chambers was a kid from Memphis making beats on a laptop. White Station High, then Middle Tennessee State, where he was still in school when a song he produced took over the country. Sicko Mode. Four number ones after that, and everybody from Drake to Beyoncé calling.

He died in June in Nashville. He was 29.

On Monday his family and the Tay Keith Legacy Foundation are putting his name on the music production room at Cloud901, the teen lab inside the Benjamin L. Hooks library in Memphis. It is free. Any teenager in the city with a library card can walk in and use it.

It is not just a plaque. The foundation is paying for a course called Beat Making 101, and the library is building an archive of his notes, his studio material and his awards so it stays in the city that made him.

"A space that gives teens the freedom to find their own voices."
