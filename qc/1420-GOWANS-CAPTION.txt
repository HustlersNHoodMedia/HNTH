Jennifer Gowans has been living with failing kidneys for 15 years. For the last three and a half she was hooked to a dialysis machine every night at home in Fort Worth, down to about 15 percent kidney function. A transplant was the only way off the machine.

Her husband Kennus got tested to give her one of his in 2023. The doctors said no. His blood pressure was too high.

He didn't argue and he didn't tell her what he decided next. "My secret goal was to re-test again." He changed how he ate, started working out, and dropped more than 20 pounds. This May he went back in. Not just a match. A perfect match. "I was crying like a baby of course."

Friday morning at 5:30 they were at Baylor Scott and White in Dallas, wheeled into two operating rooms. His left kidney is in his wife now. Doctors say both are doing well.

They have been married 26 years. Their anniversary is in April.

"The vows meant something to me. For better or for worse, richer or poorer, sickness and health."
