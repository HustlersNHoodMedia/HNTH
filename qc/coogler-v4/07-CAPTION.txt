Ryan Coogler made four movies that took in $2.4 billion.

He owned none of them.

That is not a scandal, that is just how it works. You make the thing, somebody else keeps it, and it feeds their family long after it stopped feeding yours. Anybody who ever built something for a boss knows the feeling.

So when he wrote Sinners, he wrote it for his uncle James, the man who took the seat his grandfathers never got to sit in and raised him on Delta blues. James died in 2015.

He walked into Hollywood with three terms and would not move on the third one: in 25 years, the movie comes back to him.

Studio after studio passed. One said yes.

Sinners made about $370 million, pulled a record 16 Oscar nominations, and won him the first Academy Award of his life.

In 2050 the film stops being theirs and becomes his. His three kids will be grown by then.

A check feeds you once. Owning it feeds who comes after you.
