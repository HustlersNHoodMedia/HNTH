Dr. Adedeji Adeniyi sprinted to his gate at the Seattle airport and made it inside United's own boarding cutoff. He even took a photo of the screen to prove it. The agent closed the door anyway and gave his seat to a standby passenger.

He got a $1,083 check for being bumped and a seat on the next morning's flight. Halfway to Newark, the crew asked if there was a doctor on board. A young man had passed out. He got him into the galley, raised his legs, tracked his heart on his Apple Watch and stayed with him for 45 minutes until he was stable.

On the way home, United changed his flight again and added a stop in San Francisco. On that leg another passenger lost consciousness, and he jumped in again and kept him stable until EMTs met the plane.

He's 30, a resident at the University of Washington, and he says he's still fighting United over how they bumped him, but he's glad he was on those planes.
