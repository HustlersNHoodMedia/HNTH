Aralean Brown raised five kids in St. Louis and kept the door open for more. She was a foster parent and an adoptive parent, and her son says the house was always full of somebody else's child who needed one.

Her husband died when Sterling was 10. She kept going.

In 2018 she was diagnosed with ALS. By that October she could no longer speak. Her son says her mind is as sharp as it ever was, and that she is still the most positive person in any room she is in.

So he put her name on something permanent. The Aralean Brown Older Youth Readiness Fund, built with the Foster and Adoptive Care Coalition in St. Louis, goes to young people ages 13 to 24 who are aging out of the system: rent, trade school, tuition, a bed, the things a family would normally cover.

Half of the homeless population in this country and 70 percent of the prison population came out of foster care. At 18 the placement just ends.

"As we ascend, as we climb together, make sure that you pull other people up with you."
