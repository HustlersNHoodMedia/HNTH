Jamel Workman has been a master barber for 20 years. His shop, Workman Gentleman's Shoppe on Market Street in West Philly, has been open since 2009. Walk past the chairs and through the back and you find the other business, J Workman Millinery. One man, one room, and the same tools hat makers used a hundred years ago.

Philadelphia is where the cowboy hat was born. Stetson was built here. By the 60s the old milliners were dying off and the craft was going with them.

Jamel made his first hat in 2014, for himself. Then he went to American Hats in Frankford, one of the last hat factories in the country, and learned how to block a hat over a wooden form with steam. Rabbit or beaver felt from the Poconos, sweatband, lining, trim. Three to five weeks per hat. They start at $500.

This summer he became the first man featured as a designer at the Great American Hat Show in Harlem.

"The hat is what completes the story."
