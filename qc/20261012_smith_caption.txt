Patrick Smith is 47. Two years ago he walked out of federal prison after 13 years on a life sentence. Now the government is trying to put him back.

In 2010 he got caught up in a crack and marijuana ring on the Tennessee-Alabama line. He turned down a plea deal for 12 to 14 years and went to trial. With his record from his teens, the law forced a life sentence. It was the longest sentence out of all 20 people in the case, longer than the man who ran it. The judge said her hands were tied.

His mama died during his first year inside. The trip home for the funeral cost $1,000, and he couldn't ask his family for it.

In 2024 a judge looked at how much the law had changed and let him go. He moved in with his sister in Alabama, picked up two jobs and started showing up to his nieces' and nephews' games.

In May the Supreme Court ruled that changes like that can't be used to free people early. Last month prosecutors filed to undo his release. About a dozen people across the country are in the same spot.

"Every time I get a call, I'm hoping it's some good news," he said.
