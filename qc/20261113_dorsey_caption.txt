At 95, James Dorsey finally has his birth certificate. He was born on Ellerbe Plantation outside Shreveport, Louisiana, in 1931, delivered by a midwife, and nobody ever wrote his birth down. He picked cotton as a kid, moved to Houston in the '50s, and only found out the paper didn't exist the day he went to get married. Not having it eventually ended his trucking career.

His daughter filed application after application with the state and got nothing back. After the story aired on local news, attorney Raynique Keelen stepped in and took his case for free. The record existed the whole time, sitting in Louisiana's files under a different name and a different date of birth. This week she walked into his living room and put the certificate in his hands.

"I don't know how to feel. I feel so good."
