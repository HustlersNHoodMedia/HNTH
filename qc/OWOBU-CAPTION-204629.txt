Max Owobu grew up in the Bronx. His father is Nigerian and was raised in Ghana, and from the time he was 15 Max spent his summers there.

That's where he kept seeing kids his own age selling things on the street or working with their families instead of sitting in a classroom.

The first one he did something about was a boy named Matthew, the son of a family friend. Max started paying for his school with money he had saved from his weekly allowance. The pictures that came back showed Matthew smiling with new friends.

Then Max and two of his boys from school went and asked family, friends and their own high school for help. They raised about $17,000 and turned it into the Yenko Foundation.

Today 10 kids at Calvary Methodist Elementary in Accra go to school on that money. A thousand dollars a year covers each one's tuition, uniform, transportation, meals and supplies.

Max says the seed was his great-aunt Ida, who made sure all 12 of her brothers and sisters made it to college.
