Omarion Calloway was 10 years old in Montgomery when he found the fire in his own house and called 911. The family lost almost everything and went from place to place after that.

In the middle of it he was the one taking care of his grandmother Alma Jean, bathing her and working her oxygen tubes, and taking care of his uncle, who was paralyzed from the neck down. Meals, medicine, the insulin shots for his uncle's diabetes, his little brother. His mother was working two jobs and he covered the hours she could not.

He did not have a word for any of it.

"I really just thought what I was doing was OK and needed to be done. I should not have had to do that, especially at 10 years old."

His grandmother died first. His uncle died before his senior year. Eight years of caring for two people, and both were gone before he walked across the stage.

He kept a 4.0. Twenty-one schools took him and he got over $1.3 million in scholarships. He went to NYU because his grandmother had put it in his head before she passed, along with the line he still repeats. A clenched fist cannot plant a seed.

He is 23 now, studying film, and making a short documentary about kids who grew up doing what he did. He called it Hands Too Small.
