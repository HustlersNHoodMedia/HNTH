Darryl Bell grew up in Kansas City, cooked in some of the best kitchens in the country, and spent two years turning an old auto shop in Napa into his own barbecue spot, Stateline Road Smokehouse.

This summer SpaceX asked him to cook dinner for 425 of its employees in Palo Alto, once a month for six months. No deposit, no written contract, just a promise to pay within 30 days. He bought $7,000 in carts and pans, rented a U-Haul, and his crew worked 14 hour shifts on their days off.

Two dinners later, SpaceX still owes him about $70,000.

In September he left a voicemail saying he might need a lawyer. Within the hour he got two messages. One said the payment had started. The other said the rest of the dinners were on hold. He has now been told three times the money is coming.

He still can't pay the crew that cooked those dinners.

"That's not even a penny for them."
