In 1984, Todd Weaver was 12 years old, living in Hazelwood, Missouri. His dad left when he was 3. His mama was battling diabetes and cancer.

So he walked onto a construction site and asked the man in charge for a job.

That man was Bob Clark, who had just started a construction company called Clayco. He paid Todd $4 an hour to pull weeds and pick up trash, on one condition: he had to stay in school and do his homework.

Todd kept that job through high school. Clark helped with his mama's bills, and Todd ended up living with the Clark family much of the time. He became the first in either family to go to college.

In 2002 he started his own company with a $150,000 loan from Clark and paid it back in 6 months. In 2008 he was $2.5 million in debt and close to losing everything. He refused to ask Clark for a bailout and raised the money himself.

Today his companies employ 585 people, build and service data centers for Google, Microsoft and Apple, and are on track to bring in about $1 billion this year.

"I knew I needed to do something to help my mom."
