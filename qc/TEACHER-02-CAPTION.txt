Second day of the school year at Wheatley High in Houston's Fifth Ward.

Darius Williams gave his senior class two paragraphs to read and one sentence to finish. He'd walked them through it. He'd annotated it with them. He'd left four words for them to fill in and had already given them the answers.

They couldn't do it.

He filmed himself on his lunch break, crying. "These are 17 and 18-year-old kids, and they couldn't fill in four words. Four words." Then: "I have seniors who simply cannot read, and seniors who cannot write."

The video hit 4.9 million views.

Two days later the story turned. Wheatley celebrated its first ever "A" rating from the state of Texas. Students walked past the sign that morning and told reporters he was wrong about them, though some admitted friends of theirs do struggle. The district said students there have made significant gains. The superintendent's answer to the video was one line: "It was worse four years ago."

Then people started digging into Williams himself. He's better known online as a chef with a big following, and Georgia's attorney general settled with a credit repair business he ran for $147,750 back in 2019.

So the conversation stopped being about four words on a page.
