Mellody Hobson was the youngest of six, raised by a single mother on the South Side of Chicago. Her mama flipped run down apartments and had too good a heart to put anybody out, so the money never came. The lights got cut. The phone got cut. The car got repossessed. They got evicted more than once, and Mellody says she never let a friend see where she lived.

She was mortified every time. She also says it's the reason she got obsessed with money: not making it, understanding it.

Princeton. Then Ariel Investments, the first Black owned mutual fund company in the country, where she is now co-CEO. Chair of Starbucks. Part owner of the Broncos.

Yesterday in Los Angeles she and her husband George Lucas opened the Lucas Museum of Narrative Art. A billion dollars, 300,000 square feet, 40,000 works. They shook hands with the first 100 people through the door.

"This is a museum of the people's art. This art belongs to everyone."
