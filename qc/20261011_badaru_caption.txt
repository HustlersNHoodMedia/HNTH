Usman Badaru was 13 when he moved to Providence with his dad and his older sister. His dad loved the city. None of them knew how anything worked yet, not housing, not school.

They ended up at Crossroads, the shelter downtown. A day or two they slept in his dad's car. Then the family floor at Crossroads, then low-income housing.

His sister wanted to be a nurse, so he wanted to be one too. After a year in public school he got into the Rhode Island Nurses Institute, a charter high school that puts kids on a fast track into healthcare.

Today he works nights in the neuro ICU at Rhode Island Hospital, taking care of patients with brain and nerve emergencies. He has been there three years. He spends his time off mentoring the kids at the same school, and this fall he was named to its board.

"I always think about where I would be if I did not come to RINI."
