Floyd Norman finally got his Oscar.

Right out of high school he walked into Disney's Burbank studio with a stack of drawings. In 1956 he started there as an apprentice on Sleeping Beauty and became the studio's first Black animator. He worked on 101 Dalmatians, The Sword in the Stone, Mary Poppins and The Jungle Book, the only feature he made directly with Walt Disney himself. Later his own company made animation for Sesame Street and the opening titles of Soul Train, and Pixar brought him in for Toy Story 2 and Monsters, Inc.

On Sunday night in Hollywood, at 91, Mahershala Ali handed him an honorary Academy Award while his wife and five children watched from the audience.

He remembered a critic who once said a documentary about his life didn't have enough drama. "On occasion, even movies have happy endings," he said. "Here's my happy ending."
