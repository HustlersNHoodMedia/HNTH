In December 1970 Aretha Franklin told Jet she wanted to put up the bond for Angela Davis, who was sitting in a New York jail waiting to be sent back to California.

Aretha was 28 and at the top of her career. Davis was 26, on the FBI's most wanted list a few weeks earlier, and facing charges she was later acquitted of. The two women had never met.

Her own father, Rev. C.L. Franklin, told her she didn't know what she was doing. She said she respected him and she was doing it anyway.

"I have the money; I got it from Black people, they've made me financially able to have it, and I want to use it in ways that will help our people."

The court never let her post it. The bond ended up coming from a farmer in Fresno who put up his land.

She spent years quietly covering payroll for the movement. Jesse Jackson said she did eleven shows for free and put gas in the vans.
