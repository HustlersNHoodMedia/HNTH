Jaloni was 14 when gun violence put him in a wheelchair in 2023.

That was only the beginning. Over the next three years he spent more than 475 days at a children's hospital in St. Louis. The first time he went home, his family couldn't keep up with his care, and he came back with bedsores and an infection. Then his grandma, the one raising him, passed while he was still in the hospital. With nowhere safe to go, his room became his home. His name went up on the door. Nurses braided his hair, a cook made him special meals, and the staff even threw a prom for the kids on his floor.

"This is my kingdom," he said from his hospital bed.

Then his aunt Deborah, the one he calls Tete, stepped up. She moved into a first-floor apartment with a wheelchair ramp and brought him home in May.

He's 18 now. He's learning to cook and handle his own meds, and he wants to get his GED, learn a trade and start his own business.
