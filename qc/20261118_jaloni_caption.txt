Jaloni was 14 when gun violence put him in a wheelchair in 2023. By then he had already lost his older sister and his 17-year-old brother the same way.

Over the next three years he spent more than 475 days at a children's hospital in St. Louis. His grandma, the one raising him since he was little, passed in 2024 while he was still inside. A social worker offered him a placement in Chicago, 300 miles away. He said no.

So the hospital became home. His name went up on the door in big letters. Nurses braided his hair, and when the staff threw a prom he taught himself to tie a tie off YouTube.

"This is my kingdom," he said from his bed.

He went to that prom with his aunt Deborah, the one he calls Tete. It was her first prom too. She took care of him the first year of his life, and she stepped up again. She moved into a first-floor apartment with a ramp, then found a bigger place and gave him the big bedroom. She brought him home in May.

He's 18 now, learning to cook and handle his own meds, and he wants his GED, a trade and his own business.
