Za'Darius Smith got a call from his mother's doctor. She didn't have much longer.

He told the Eagles. They didn't want to let him go. Five games into his eleventh season, three Pro Bowls and 70 sacks behind him, he retired. That was the only way out he had. His mama died soon after. Then his grandmother. Then his father.

Her name was Sharon. She worked as a correctional officer at Butler County in Alabama for 22 years. When he was little she used to take him inside the jail so he could see men he knew locked up.

In 2019 he signed with Green Bay. Before he ever reported, he drove 14 hours from Baltimore to her house in Greenville, Alabama, and asked her if she was ready to stop working. She told him her bag was already packed. That's the picture on the left. She's still in uniform.

He signed with Atlanta this month. Not for the money, the Browns called first. Atlanta is the closest team to home, and his mama always wanted the family to be able to drive up and watch him play.

When he suits up at Mercedes-Benz Stadium he's renting two Greyhound buses and parking them at her church.

"That's who my mama always wanted to come."
