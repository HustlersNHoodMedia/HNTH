Tommie Smith won the 200 meters at the 1968 Olympics in Mexico City with a world record. On the podium, during the anthem, he and John Carlos raised a black-gloved fist.

The U.S. Olympic Committee suspended them that night and gave them 48 hours to leave the Olympic Village. One of its directors said they made the country "look like the devil." Back home came the death threats.

Smith is 82 now and lives in the Atlanta area. For more than a decade he and his wife Delois have run the Tommie Smith Youth Initiative in DeKalb County, working to stop young people from losing their lives to violence.

Channel 2 sat down with him this week. "My life was threatened more than once, but this was something that had to be done," he said.

Delois, who is also the mother of singer Montell Jordan, put it this way: "We wanted them to know that you can be something, you can do something."
