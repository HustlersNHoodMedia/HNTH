A fire tore through Sand Branch, Texas on Saturday night and burned 10 acres. There was not one fire hydrant in the whole community, so when the flames came back up, neighbors went at them with the plastic bottles of drinking water they keep in the house. Nothing has come out of the taps in Sand Branch since the 1980s.

That bottled water is not for emergencies. It is all they have. Sand Branch was founded after the Civil War as a refuge for people freed from slavery, and it is the last unincorporated community left in Dallas County. Its wells were condemned in the 1980s after they tested positive for E. coli, and the hundred or so people still there have gotten by on bottles for drinking, cooking and bathing ever since. Forty-one years. The city's wastewater treatment plant sits 400 feet away.

The water that finally put the fire out had to be trucked in from Dallas and nine other departments. It took until Monday. It was the third fire in Sand Branch in two weeks.

Derek Avery runs the coalition fighting to get pipes into those homes.

"We have a fire that should have been put out in an hour."
