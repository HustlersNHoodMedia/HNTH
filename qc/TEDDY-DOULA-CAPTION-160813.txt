When Teddy Gant was 12 he asked his mama how he came into the world. She told him his heart rate dropped with every contraction and the doctors had to cut him out in an emergency C-section before he flatlined.

He never forgot it.

Years later he was working up to 70 hours a week as a bank security officer in Houston and taking birth worker classes on the side. In 2024 he left the bank for good and went full time as a doula and massage therapist.

Now he is in delivery rooms with Black mothers, the ones who need somebody in their corner the most. In Texas, Black women are twice as likely as white women to die from pregnancy and childbirth.

He trains new doulas too, and his story is in a PBS documentary called Teddy.

"When you're around me, just know that you should feel safe."
