Daniel Cressy is 23, from Metairie, and he has wanted to fly planes since he was a kid. Sickle cell took that off the table. He was born with it. The pain sent him to emergency rooms and hospital beds when he should have been in class.

In 2022 he applied to the FAA for the medical certificate every pilot needs. They turned him down because of the disease. He asked them what it would take. The answer was simple and impossible at the same time: cure it.

So he went and got it cured. Gene therapy, two years of waiting on approvals, his own stem cells shipped to Scotland to be edited and shipped back, chemo, six weeks in the hospital. He kept a flight simulator set up in his room the whole time and flew it from the bed.

In June he rang the bell as the first person in Louisiana and the Gulf South cured of sickle cell through gene editing. This week the FAA cleared him.

"There's nothing holding me back now."
