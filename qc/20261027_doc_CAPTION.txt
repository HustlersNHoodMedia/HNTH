The D.O.C. was 21 and four months into his first album, No One Can Do It Better, when he fell asleep driving home from a party in November 1989. He got thrown out the back window of his car. The crash wrecked his vocal cords, and that voice never came back.

He says only one person told him the truth about it. Dr. Dre told him, "Don't rap. You don't sound good."

So he kept writing. He wrote for N.W.A, he wrote on The Chronic for Dre, and he was in the studio with Snoop on Doggystyle.

"This voice is the only voice my kids know," he told Rolling Stone.

This past weekend in Las Vegas, Nas and the Paid In Full Foundation honored him with a five-year, $500K grant, and Ice Cube was the one who brought him to the stage.
