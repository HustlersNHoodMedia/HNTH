Omarion Calloway cannot remember a time when he was not taking care of somebody. He was cooking for the family before he was a teenager. At 10 he was bathing his grandmother in Montgomery and working her oxygen tubes. When a stroke left his uncle paralyzed from the neck down, he was one of the hands lifting him.

He did not have a word for any of it. He thought that was just what a good grandson does.

"I really just thought what I was doing was OK and needed to be done. I should not have had to do that, especially at 10 years old. No child should ever have to carry adult responsibilities at that age."

There are 5.4 million kids and teenagers in this country doing the same thing right now, and Black boys are some of the least counted. Teachers see a kid asleep at his desk or missing homework and write him off, and nobody asks what he was doing at 4 in the morning.

He is 23 now and finishing at NYU.
