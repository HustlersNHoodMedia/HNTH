Dolvett Quince, the trainer from The Biggest Loser, was a little boy in Stamford, Connecticut in 1978 when he and his sister and two brothers went into foster care.

They got split up, and he was scared he'd never see them again. One social worker fought to get them placed with the same foster family so they could stay together.

Then one day she asked him his favorite color. He said orange. The next time he saw her, she handed him an orange book she made by hand. His photos, the time he was born, his weight, the hospital, a family tree so he'd know where he came from, and blank pages at the end so he could keep adding to it.

On the cover she wrote: "This is my book, and this is me!!"

40 years later, he still has it. He calls it his "It's Gonna Be Okay" book. When somebody asks about his grandparents, he looks it up in there.

He recently found a name inside that he believes is hers, Lois Huntziger, and now he's trying to find her or her family.

"I don't know why you did it. I don't know how many times you've done it. But I'm grateful you did it for me."
