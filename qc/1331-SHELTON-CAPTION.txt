Arthur Ashe won the U.S. Open in 1968 and reached the final again in 1972. After that, no Black American man got back to a U.S. Open final. Fifty four years.

Friday night Ben Shelton and Frances Tiafoe played the first Grand Slam semifinal between two Black American men in the Open Era. It happened inside Arthur Ashe Stadium, on the court named after the man who did it last.

Shelton won it. First Grand Slam final of his career.

Earlier that same day Taylor Townsend and Katerina Siniakova took the women's doubles title, which completed a career Grand Slam for Townsend as a team.

"I also just take a lot of pride in this, being a Black woman here. It's two Black women here in this finals. There are two Black men in the semifinals. And this is a phenomenal time for tennis, for inspiration, for representation."

Ashe did not just win. He spent his life opening the door for the kids coming behind him. Fifty four years later two of them played each other for a spot in that final, in his house.
