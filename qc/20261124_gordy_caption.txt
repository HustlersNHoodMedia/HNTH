In January 1959, Berry Gordy was a 29-year-old songwriter in Detroit who wanted his own record label. He went to the Ber-Berry Co-op, the savings fund his family paid into every month, and asked for $1,000.

His big sister Esther ran the fund, and she wasn't sold. Berry called her bossy. After some back and forth, the family voted to lend him $800 at 6% interest, and he had to sign a note for it.

He paid every dollar back. That label became Motown, and the house on West Grand Boulevard where he started it became Hitsville U.S.A.

Esther went on to hold some of the top jobs at Motown. In 1985 she turned that house into the Motown Museum, and the loan papers have been on display there.

Berry sold Motown in 1988 for $61 million. He turns 97 this Friday.
