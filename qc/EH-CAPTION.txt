Elliott Hall's dad worked at Ford's Rouge plant in Detroit. When Elliott asked about a summer job there, his dad told him no. He was scared that once his son started making Ford money, he'd never go back to school.

In 1956, right out of high school, a manager at the Detroit News promised Elliott a spot riding the newspaper trucks. Then he took it back: "The union won't accept colored boys; I can't get you a job."

The next summer a letter came from Eastern Michigan University. His grades weren't good enough to come back. He was painting the family house with his dad and brothers when the mailman brought it, and he stuffed it in his pocket and never showed anybody.

He went to junior college, then Wayne State, and walked out with a law degree in 1965, the first in his family to finish college. In 1987 Ford made him a vice president, and he went to his father's grave to tell him.

Elliott is 88 now. He just put the whole story in a book, "From Begole Street to Ford's C-Suite."
