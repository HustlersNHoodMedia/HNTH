Nathaniel, Minnie, Eddie Mae, Edward, Lonnie, Mildred, Thomas and Deloris. The Davis siblings of Hattiesburg, Mississippi.

Their parents married in 1926. Their father worked more than 20 years at the Hercules Powder Company with a fourth-grade education, while their mother ran a home of ten where everyone had a job, from the garden to the livestock. They were the first family in their neighborhood to get electric lights. The kids grew up through the Great Depression, and every single one of them graduated from DePriest Consolidated High School, the first consolidated school for Black students in Forrest County. "There was no wonder if you were going to school or not," says Deloris, 80, the youngest. "That was not our decision."

Guinness World Records has certified all eight as the oldest living group of eight siblings on Earth: 721 years and 282 days between them. Nathaniel, the oldest, turns 98 this week. Several of them are veterans. All of them are still active in their communities.

Nathaniel says the record is just one piece of the plan their parents created 100 years ago: "What we have going as a family will continue for generations."
