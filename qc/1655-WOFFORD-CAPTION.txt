Burgin, Kentucky has about a thousand people in it. A developer named Panattoni wants to put a hyperscale data center on land just north of town, and to do it the city first has to annex that land.

Tuesday night the council met in the fire department engine bay. More than a hundred residents packed in, some carrying their own lawn chairs, a lot of them in blue shirts for the group We Are Mercer County. They talked about the noise, the air, the water, the power it would pull.

"There is every reason not to rush. We are clearly a target. Why? Because the land is inexpensive, the power is nearby and cheap, and the local government is eager for an increase in tax base. But we are not interested in being sacrificed."

The council was about to vote on having the city attorney start drafting the annexation ordinance. One member had already recused himself. Another was absent.

Then Melinda Wofford got up and walked out the door.

That left the council without a quorum. No quorum, no business. The meeting was over.

"You can't do business without a quorum," she said afterward, standing in the gravel lot.

It was the second time the town had come this close to handing over that land.
