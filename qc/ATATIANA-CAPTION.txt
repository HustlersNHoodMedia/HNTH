On October 12, 2019, James Smith was sitting on his porch in south Fort Worth when he noticed his neighbor's front door open at two in the morning. He walked across the street to look for himself. The lights were on, nothing looked wrong, so he called the non-emergency line and asked for a welfare check.

Dispatch sent it out as an "open structure." Officer Aaron Dean walked into the backyard, never announced himself, and fired through the bedroom window. Atatiana Jefferson, 28, died in front of her 8-year-old nephew Zion. She was watching him that night because his mother and her own mother were both in the hospital.

Smith had lived on that block for 50 years. "I made the call," he said afterward. "I made the call because I thought they were going to do what I called them to do."

Two weeks after her funeral, her father died. Her mother died that January in the same house. Her sister Amber, Zion's mom, died in 2023 at 33.

On August 25, 2026, the Fort Worth City Council voted unanimously to pay Atatiana's estate $11.25 million. Two weeks before that vote, the same council voted 7-4 to raise the cap on legal fees for the attorney defending Dean in her family's own lawsuit, from $100,000 to $200,000.

Smith spoke at that meeting holding a picture of her.
