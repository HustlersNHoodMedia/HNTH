Cobb County held its back to school kickoff for staff in late July. The superintendent, Chris Ragsdale, spoke for close to an hour.

He got to the part about the books the district pulled off library shelves. According to teachers who were in the room, he tied those books to child sex trafficking, and told the educators sitting in front of him that if they didn't agree with him they were complicit in it.

More than a hundred of them stood up and walked out.

Five days later, teachers at eight different schools got pulled out of class and put in a room with their principal and a district administrator. Two questions. Why did you walk out of the superintendent's speech, and who gave you permission?

The union says close to 200 have been questioned. They could be written up for insubordination or unprofessionalism.

"They are being interviewed and possibly facing consequences for standing up for themselves, and we will stand up for them," said Jeff Hubbard, president of the Cobb County Association of Educators.

These are grown adults being asked who let them get out of a chair.
