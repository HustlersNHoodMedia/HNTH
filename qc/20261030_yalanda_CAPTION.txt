Yalanda Williams bought a brand-new house in Douglasville, Georgia, in 2017. She says she bought new because she thought it would be safe for her and her family.

About a year later, the foundation problems started.

Since then, multiple engineers have found structural defects making parts of the house sink. A county inspection this summer listed split studs, foundation cracks and sagging drywall. In the basement, metal bars are coming through the concrete wall.

She says she has spent $72K over four years, much of it from her life savings, on engineers and lawyers. Legal documents put the repairs at more than $500K.

She says the builder offered her about $27K. Her court case got sent to arbitration, and now she can't afford another attorney. In an October 15 email, the builder said it would not negotiate directly with her while she represents herself.

"Either I find $534,000 to repair my house, or I walk away," she said.
