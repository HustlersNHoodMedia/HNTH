Breakfast was already on the table. Kailene Curran Herndon sat down and the next thing she remembers is the hospital.

Her seizure knocked over the highchair her baby daughter was sitting in. Her 7-year-old, Zayden, picked the baby up off the floor, put her on the dining room table, and got on the phone with 911. He gave the dispatcher the address, answered every question, and stayed on the line.

Then he sat with his mother and told her, "It's OK mommy, it's OK."

Here's the part that isn't luck. Kailene has a history of seizures, and she'd been running through this with him since he was two years old. "We've practiced a lot just because we never know if and when it's going to happen," she said. "Calling 911 isn't a game."

On August 11, Virginia Beach police handed him the Citizen Lifesaving Award. The officer who nominated him said what stood out wasn't just the call. It was that a 7-year-old thought about his sister first.

Somebody sat him down and taught him. That's the whole story.
