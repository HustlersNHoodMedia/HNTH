Freddie Jackson went home to rest Monday night, three days after his birthday.

Before the whole world slow-danced to his voice, he was a church choir kid from Harlem singing background and demos for Melba Moore, Evelyn "Champagne" King and Angela Bofill. He did not get a record of his own until 1985.

Then Rock Me Tonight went number one, and for the next seven years he barely left the top of the chart. Ten number one R&B records. Nineteen top tens. The soundtrack of just about every Black household of the 80s.

His family closed their goodbye with a word to the fans: "To everyone who ever sang along, danced or felt something when Freddie took the stage: thank you."
