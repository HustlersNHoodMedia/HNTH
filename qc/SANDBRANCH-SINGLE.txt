A fire tore through Sand Branch, Texas on Saturday night and burned 10 acres before crews got it contained. There was not one fire hydrant in the whole community. The water had to be trucked in from Dallas and nine other departments, and firefighters worked through the night and into Monday.

Sand Branch was founded after the Civil War as a refuge for people freed from slavery. It is the last unincorporated community left in Dallas County. Its wells were condemned in the 1980s, and since then the hundred or so people still there have gotten by on bottled water for drinking, cooking and bathing. No sewer lines either. The city's wastewater treatment plant sits 400 feet away.

When the wells went bad, families were offered buyouts. The county judge says most homeowners ended up with about $350 to go find somewhere else to live. The ones who stayed are the ones who watched this fire come.

Johnny Chambers has lived there since 1988.

"There's been so many times, everybody be coming here talking, talking, nothing was ever done."
