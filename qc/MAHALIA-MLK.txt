The night before the March on Washington, Martin Luther King was still at the Willard Hotel working on his speech. He finished around three in the morning. The word "dream" was not in it.

Clarence Jones, who helped write it, was about fifteen yards behind him at the Lincoln Memorial. King was reading from the page, the part about America writing a bad check.

Then Mahalia Jackson called out from behind him. She had already sung that afternoon and she was King's favorite singer, the one he'd call on the phone when he was low.

"Tell them about the dream, Martin."

Jones watched King look over at her, slide the prepared text to the side of the lectern, grip it with both hands and change his whole stance.

"These people don't know it," Jones said to the man next to him, "but they're about to go to church."

Everything everybody quotes today came after that.
