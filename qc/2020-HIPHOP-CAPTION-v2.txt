The Bronx School of Hip Hop opened this week with about 100 freshmen. Public high school, city funded, in the borough where the music started.

The building tells you where you are before anybody says a word. Murals of Lauryn, Biggie and Hov down the halls. A wall that reads THE WORLD IS YOURS, YOU MUST LEARN, SKY IS THE LIMIT. A subway car painted in the hallway. A booth in the corner of a classroom.

Jason Reyes, the founding principal, is from the Bronx and used to run a middle school. He spent years pushing for this building to exist. "It just feels like a dream come true."

It is not a rap class tacked onto a normal school. The five elements run through the subjects. In geometry, students take a mic and explain their calculations out loud. In English they read Richard Wright's Native Son next to Nas' I Can and break down what both writers are doing with language.

Xavian Sanchez came from Harlem for the first freshman class. "Just hearing hip hop being in a school, that automatically caught my eye."

"We want students to come in and feel like they're home. They're in the Bronx."

Ninth grade this year, one grade added every year until it is a full high school.
