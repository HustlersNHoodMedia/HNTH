Talib Kweli was a teenager working part time at Nkiru Books, the oldest Black-owned bookstore in Brooklyn, when he decided that if he ever had money, he'd put it into that store.

In 1998, right after the Black Star album with Mos Def, the two of them bought it. Talib's mama chaired the board. The store became the spot in Brooklyn for readings and poetry slams.

That's the store in the photo from 1999, with Talib holding his newborn daughter Diani out front.

He had to close it in 2002, with two little kids at home and a rap career to carry.

On November 1, Diani, now 27, brought Nkiru back as an online bookstore and threw the relaunch in Bed-Stuy. She hosted and performed. Her dad was on the turntables.

"My daughter made this happen," Talib said. "I love the way Diani pays it forward. And that's the way it's supposed to be."
