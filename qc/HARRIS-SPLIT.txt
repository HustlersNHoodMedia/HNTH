At 14 he had already failed two years of high school, was stealing cars, and was about to be somebody's father.

James Harris grew up in Fresno in what he calls a crack den. His father moved in and out of his life fighting an addiction. His mother was mostly gone. One night he was driving a stolen Cadillac to pick up his girlfriend Rosina, pregnant with their son, when the police tried to stop him. He made a U-turn and ran. She watched the chase from where she stood.

He got kicked out of his high school. He was working fast food and had stopped thinking about school at all.

Then a counselor at the continuation school, Henry Ray, told him something no adult ever had: that Fresno State had a program that would pay for him. Nobody had ever taken the time to tell him what existed. Harris says everybody else looked at him, judged him, and figured he didn't care.

He enrolled. He was years behind and had to take extra math and English before he could even start the classes his degree required. He kept going. When the acceptance letter from UCLA medical school came, he drove back to the continuation school with a copy in his hand so Ray could see it.

He is now chair of surgery at Johns Hopkins Howard County Medical Center and helps train the residents there. Physician of the Year in 2022. He and Rosina have been married 34 years.

He says the message isn't for the troubled kid. It's for the adults standing in front of one.
