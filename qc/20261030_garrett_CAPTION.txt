Beatrice Garrett is 70 years old, and she just gave her little brother one of her kidneys.

Kenneth Lee Walker spent nine years in the Marines and 31 years at the post office. Then his kidneys failed and he ended up on dialysis.

Twice he got the call that a donor kidney was ready, and twice it fell through. The last time, he and his wife rushed to the hospital in Memphis before sunrise, only to hear the surgeon say it couldn't be used.

After that call, his big sister told the donor program she was ready.

"My mom gave me strict instructions before she passed about what I was supposed to do as the oldest," Beatrice said. "Number one was to make sure I took care of what she called 'her babies.'"

Fifteen years ago doctors turned her down as a donor for her niece. This time her cholesterol had dropped almost 100 points, and she passed every test.

They went into surgery together on July 28. The next day she walked down the hall and saw him sitting up in a chair.

"His color, his complexion was different than the day that we went into surgery," she said.
