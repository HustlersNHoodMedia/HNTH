Ernest Harden Jr. has been working since the 1970s. You know him as Marcus, the kid who worked at George Jefferson's cleaners on The Jeffersons. Fifty years of guest spots, supporting parts and shows that came and went. Sunday night, at 73, he walked on stage at the Creative Arts Emmys and picked up the first one of his life, Outstanding Guest Actor in a Drama, for playing Louie Cloverfield on The Pitt. Colman Domingo was in his category.

He got up there and thanked his agent, his children, his castmates from The Jeffersons. Then he went back 46 years.

In 1980 he starred opposite Bette Davis in a TV movie called White Mama. She got an Emmy nomination for it. She never won.

"We didn't make it then, but this one is for us."

Backstage he put it plainly: "I am on cloud 25."
