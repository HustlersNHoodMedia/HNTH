Kwaun Shaw was 31, a husband and a dad, three years into his job with the Crowley Police Department, when a rash sent him to the doctor in June 2025. It was stage 5 kidney failure.

From then on his nights belonged to a machine. Ten hours of dialysis while he slept, every single night. And every morning he unplugged and went to work anyway.

"I plug in the machine every night for a 10-hour duration period, and then I unplug every morning before going to work."

He went public asking for a donor. Around Thanksgiving, a school counselor two towns over saw his story on the news. Alexa Nava had spent nine years at her school and had once watched a student go through kidney problems. She had never met him. She got tested anyway.

On September 21 she gave him her kidney. He's home now, getting stronger every day, and on Thursday the two of them met for the first time.

His wife Whitney put it best: "What do you say to somebody who gave you many, many, many more years with your best friend?"
