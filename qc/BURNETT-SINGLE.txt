Elizabeth Burnett, the woman everybody on the job called Queen Ann, started in construction at 18, building docks in New York. Getting into the operating engineers union took years, and she has said race and gender were both in the way. She got her apprenticeship in 1993.

When the towers came down she was on the site almost immediately, running the heavy machinery that took the pile apart. Twelve-hour shifts. Twenty-four. Thirty-six. Close to a year of it. Around 1.6 million tons of steel, concrete and human remains came out of that hole.

She kept the hard hat. Yellow, wrapped in packing tape along the brim, covered in stickers she put on it herself. The Yankees. A We Love New York heart. The seal of the operating engineers. And one that reads Ground Zero, WTC-7, Welcome to Hell, with the towers drawn in as the last two letters.

She gave it to the Smithsonian. It sits in the National Museum of African American History and Culture now, twenty-five years after she wore it.
