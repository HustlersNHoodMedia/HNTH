In at 18. Home at 30. 🔒

Eleven and a half years, one of them in solitary. Kerwin Pittman walked out in January 2018 and went straight back to Southeast Raleigh.

He had family, so he had a bed. Most of the men he came home with didn't. And nobody warns you about the small stuff. He had to learn how to drive again. How to use a debit card again.

So he built what he wished somebody had built for him. Started a nonprofit. Then put whole reentry centers inside RVs and drove them across North Carolina. First one served over 3,000 people in 70 days. 🚐

Then he saw a listing. Wayne Correctional Center, Goldsboro. 400 beds, 19 acres, sitting empty since 2013.

He walked through it. Dust everywhere, birds nesting in the ceiling, his own team looking at him like he lost it. He says he stood in there and saw people walking around free.

Offered $275,000 and told himself he wasn't going a dollar over. November, the state said yes. 🔑

Far as anybody can tell, he's the first formerly incarcerated person in this country to own a prison.

Barbed wire comes down. Bars come out. Signs that say no inmates past this point come off the wall. Private rooms instead of open dorms. 300 men at a time, six months each, learning HVAC, masonry, welding, plumbing. 🔨

And the state told him something he didn't know. That $275K he paid? It goes right back into fixing up the prisons he used to live in.
