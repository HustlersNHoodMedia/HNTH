Thirty-two years, ten months and seven days. That's how long Stephen Pina counted, and last Thursday the counting stopped.

He was convicted in 1996 for a 1993 shooting outside a public housing complex in Mission Hill. Life without parole. He said from the first day that he was misidentified, and two witnesses said otherwise.

One of them was a heroin user who put his own confidence at a 6 out of 10. The other one first told a different story and didn't pick Pina at all. She testified while she was hospitalized at Taunton State Hospital following a suicide attempt in prison. His lawyers were never told.

That, plus DNA evidence that became usable decades later, is what a judge cited in 2022 when he threw the conviction out and sent Pina home to Dorchester after 28 years inside. The state's highest court backed the judge in July. On August 13 the Suffolk district attorney's office dropped the case for good, saying it could not meet its burden at trial. Both witnesses are dead.

When the doors opened in 2022, the first thing he did was nearly tackle his son in the hallway. Both of them crying, hugging for the first time outside a prison. The son was 28 years old. Pina had been inside 28 years.

Last Thursday he came out of the courthouse and told the crowd he was vindicated. Then he found his mother, Josephine, who had watched the whole thing from the same building in 1996. Her line about the sentence her son got: life without parole isn't life, it's basically death.

Back in 1993 he was living in Georgia when he heard he was wanted. He got on the road and turned himself in. His words: I knew I didn't kill anybody, so I said let me go back, I knew they were making a mistake.
