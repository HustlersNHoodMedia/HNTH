Darlene Lewis grew up a few blocks from 11th Street in Northwest Roanoke, the street people told you to stay away from.

In 2015 she found an empty building on the corner of 11th and Moorman. "I got my first retirement check, and I paid the rent with it." She didn't have a plan yet. She opened the doors and asked the neighborhood what it needed. Food. Clothes. A safe place for the kids after school.

Two years later the landlord sent a letter giving them until the end of August to get out. The kids who came every day made signs: Don't close the Hope Center. The neighborhood showed up, and the doors stayed open.

In 2024 the Hope Center moved into the building next door, from one room to ten. It's open seven days a week. Food boxes go out every Tuesday and Saturday. A man who was living in his car got an apartment because she found out about it.

"These grown people are calling her mama," one volunteer said.

Every May she throws a block party on 11th Street. "For a few hours we get to let the community say, 'We can go to 11th Street now and we can feel safe.'"
