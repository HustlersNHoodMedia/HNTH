She finished in the top ten percent of her class and still didn't go to college. Nobody in her house knew how financial aid worked, so at 18 Shay Taylor-Allen took a job cleaning the hospital she was born in.

Ten years at Yale New Haven. Fourteen dollars an hour. Patient rooms, psychiatric units, administrative offices. She took it because people told her you could retire from a job like that.

Then her mother's lungs were burned in a house fire and the damage never healed right. Every time she went in, the doctors treated her symptoms like they were in her head. The family couldn't get an answer out of anybody.

So Shay wrote to the hospital's chief executive. She knew who he was because she cleaned his office. Within days her mother had a new medical team and a diagnosis nobody had caught: vocal cord dysfunction.

That's when she decided what she was going to be. She studied biology at Southern Connecticut State while working nights with the cart. Then a master's at Quinnipiac. More than 20 medical schools turned her down before Howard said yes.

In March she matched into anesthesiology at Yale New Haven. Same hospital. She still has her old badge. The new one says doctor.

She owes about $590,000.
