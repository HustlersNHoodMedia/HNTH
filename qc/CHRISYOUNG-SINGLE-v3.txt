He was 22 years old. Two life sentences. No parole. For dr*gs. 🔒

Chris Young came up broke in Clarksville, Tennessee. Two small charges as a teenager. Then a third case at 22, and the "three strikes" law did the rest. The judge had no say in it.

So he sat four years in a county jail waiting on trial. And he read. Over a thousand books. Economics, physics, history. Taught himself to code without ever touching a computer. Said he told himself the cell was a dorm room. 📚

Then he found out he was allowed to speak before they sentenced him. He wrote a 45-minute statement and memorized every word of it.

His reason: "I wanted to be seen. I wanted the judge to know I was a person."

Judge Kevin Sharp thought five years was fair. The law made him say life, twice over. He said it.

Then he quit. Walked away from a federal bench appointment he had for the rest of his life, and spent years fighting to get Chris out. 🤝

2021, the clemency came through.

May 16 of this year, Chris walked across the stage at SMU. Economics and public policy. 🎓 Sharp flew in from San Diego to sit in that crowd.

He's 37 now. Runs a consulting firm. Book just dropped this month.

On the man he used to be: "This Chris wouldn't exist without that Chris." 🙏
