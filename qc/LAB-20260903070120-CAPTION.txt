Jennine Labuzan had never been arrested in her life. She spent two weeks in a jail cell this year because she would not sign one piece of paper.

She started digging into her family tree after years of hearing relatives say the names of people long gone. The search turned up federal records showing her third great grandfather, Charles Augustus Labuzan, bought into 1,361 acres in Washington County, Mississippi with two partners in 1836. The patent came through in 1840. It says the land goes to heirs and assigns forever.

She paid for a title abstract herself. She filed a deed. She filed her petition in 2022 and she has represented herself the whole way.

The courts ruled against her at every level. The Fifth Circuit upheld it. The Supreme Court would not hear it. A judge ordered her to sign a quitclaim deed giving up her claim, and when she said no, the judge held her in contempt and sent the U.S. Marshals.

They moved her from Georgia to Tennessee to Mississippi. She went in on June 5. She came out on June 23 and she still had not signed.

"I've never had more than a speeding ticket, let alone jail."
