Dewontis Groomster Sr. knew something was wrong with his son before anybody in a white coat did. Bruises that kept showing up. A baby who was not acting like himself. "We kept going to the hospital in Montgomery. They kept saying he was fine."

It took almost two years and a referral to Children's of Alabama in Birmingham to get the answer. Wiskott-Aldrich Syndrome, a genetic condition so rare it hits about three in a million boys. His immune system did not work and his blood did not clot right. The only real fix was new bone marrow.

They found a donor. It fell through. Then a stranger in Oklahoma named Carolyn Daniels came up as a match and said yes. "I didn't know what I was getting into, but I knew I had a chance to help somebody, so I'm gonna do it."

Little Dewontis got the transplant at 3. His body fought it. His tongue swelled so bad he could not talk for six months and had to be fed through a tube. He and his father lived inside that hospital for seven months.

He is 5 now. He plays football and basketball. And last week, for the first time, he got to hug the woman whose marrow is keeping him alive.

"I've never met him before today, but I feel like I know him."
